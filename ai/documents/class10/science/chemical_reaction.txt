TOPIC: Chemical Reaction

A chemical reaction takes place when the nature and identity of
one or more substances change, resulting in the formation of new
substances.

Signs that a chemical reaction has taken place include:

1. Change in state
2. Change in colour
3. Evolution of a gas
4. Change in temperature

Examples:

1. Milk left at room temperature during summer changes.
2. Iron exposed to a humid atmosphere undergoes rusting.
3. Grapes undergo fermentation.
4. Food is cooked and digested.
5. Respiration takes place in our body.  

---

TOPIC: Chemical Equation

A chemical equation represents a chemical reaction using
chemical symbols and chemical formulae.

Example:

Magnesium + Oxygen → Magnesium oxide

Mg + O₂ → MgO

The substances that undergo chemical change are called
**reactants**.

The new substances formed are called **products**.

Reactants are written on the left-hand side (LHS).

Products are written on the right-hand side (RHS).

An arrow (→) shows the direction in which reactants change
into products. 

---

TOPIC: Word Equation

A word equation represents a chemical reaction using the names
of the reactants and products.

Example:

Magnesium + Oxygen → Magnesium oxide

Reactants → Products

---

TOPIC: Skeletal Chemical Equation

A chemical equation in which the number of atoms of each
element is not the same on both sides is called an
**unbalanced or skeletal chemical equation**.

Example:

Mg + O₂ → MgO

This equation is unbalanced because the number of oxygen atoms
is different on the two sides. 

---

TOPIC: Balanced Chemical Equation

A chemical equation in which the number of atoms of every
element is the same on both the reactant side and product side
is called a balanced chemical equation.

Chemical equations must be balanced according to the
**law of conservation of mass**.

Mass can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction.

Example:

2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO

---

TOPIC: Law of Conservation of Mass

The total mass of the elements present in the products must be
equal to the total mass of the elements present in the reactants.

Therefore, the number of atoms of each element remains the same
before and after a chemical reaction. 

---

TOPIC: Balancing Chemical Equations

Chemical equations are balanced by using coefficients.

While balancing an equation:

1. Do not change the chemical formula of a substance.
2. Change only the coefficients placed before the formulae.
3. Use the smallest whole-number coefficients.
4. Finally, count the atoms on both sides to check the equation.

Example:

Unbalanced equation:

Fe + H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + H₂

Balanced equation:

3Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂

This method is called the **hit-and-trial method**.  

---

TOPIC: Physical State Symbols in Chemical Equations

Physical states can be represented along with chemical formulae.

(s) = Solid

(l) = Liquid

(g) = Gas

(aq) = Aqueous solution, meaning dissolved in water

Example:

3Fe(s) + 4H₂O(g) → Fe₃O₄(s) + 4H₂(g)

Reaction conditions such as temperature, pressure and catalysts
may also be written above or below the reaction arrow. 

---

TOPIC: Types of Chemical Reactions

The major types of chemical reactions discussed are:

1. Combination Reaction
2. Decomposition Reaction
3. Displacement Reaction
4. Double Displacement Reaction
5. Oxidation and Reduction Reactions

Chemical reactions involve the breaking and making of bonds
between atoms to produce new substances. 

---

TOPIC: Combination Reaction

A reaction in which two or more substances combine to form a
single product is called a combination reaction.

General form:

A + B → AB

Example:

CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + Heat

Calcium oxide + Water → Calcium hydroxide

Another example:

C + O₂ → CO₂

Another example:

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

 

---

TOPIC: Exothermic Reaction

A reaction in which heat is released along with the formation of
products is called an exothermic reaction.

Examples:

1. Burning of natural gas

CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O

2. Respiration

C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Energy

3. Decomposition of vegetable matter into compost

In an exothermic reaction, the reaction mixture may become warm. 

---

TOPIC: Respiration as an Exothermic Reaction

During respiration, glucose combines with oxygen in the cells
of our body and releases energy.

Equation:

C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Energy

Therefore, respiration is an exothermic process.

---

TOPIC: Whitewashing Reaction

Calcium oxide reacts with water to form slaked lime.

CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂

Slaked lime reacts slowly with carbon dioxide in air to form
calcium carbonate.

Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O

Calcium carbonate forms a thin layer on the walls and gives
them a shiny finish. 

---

TOPIC: Decomposition Reaction

A reaction in which a single reactant breaks down to form two
or more simpler products is called a decomposition reaction.

General form:

AB → A + B

Decomposition reactions are the opposite of combination
reactions.



---

TOPIC: Thermal Decomposition Reaction

A decomposition reaction caused by heating is called a thermal
decomposition reaction.

Example:

2FeSO₄ → Fe₂O₃ + SO₂ + SO₃

Another example:

CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂

Here, calcium carbonate decomposes on heating to form calcium
oxide and carbon dioxide.

Calcium oxide is also called quick lime. 

---

TOPIC: Decomposition by Electricity

Some decomposition reactions take place when electrical energy
is supplied.

Example:

Water decomposes into hydrogen and oxygen during electrolysis.

2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂

The decomposition requires electrical energy.

---

TOPIC: Decomposition by Light

Some compounds decompose when exposed to sunlight.

Example:

2AgCl → 2Ag + Cl₂

White silver chloride turns grey because silver metal is formed.

Another example:

2AgBr → 2Ag + Br₂

These reactions are used in black-and-white photography.  

---

TOPIC: Endothermic Reaction

A reaction in which energy is absorbed is called an
endothermic reaction.

Decomposition reactions generally require energy in the form of:

1. Heat
2. Light
3. Electricity

Examples include decomposition reactions caused by heating,
sunlight and electrolysis. 

---

TOPIC: Displacement Reaction

A reaction in which a more reactive element displaces another
element from its compound is called a displacement reaction.

General form:

A + BC → AC + B

Example:

Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu

Iron displaces copper from copper sulphate solution.

Other examples:

Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu

Pb + CuCl₂ → PbCl₂ + Cu

Zinc and lead are more reactive than copper and can displace it
from its compounds. 

---

TOPIC: Double Displacement Reaction

A reaction in which ions or groups of atoms are exchanged
between two reactants is called a double displacement reaction.

General form:

AB + CD → AD + CB

Example:

Na₂SO₄ + BaCl₂ → BaSO₄ + 2NaCl

Here, barium sulphate is formed as an insoluble solid. 

---

TOPIC: Precipitation Reaction

A reaction that produces an insoluble substance called a
precipitate is known as a precipitation reaction.

Example:

Na₂SO₄(aq) + BaCl₂(aq) → BaSO₄(s) + 2NaCl(aq)

Barium sulphate is an insoluble white precipitate. 

---

TOPIC: Oxidation

A substance is said to be oxidised when it:

1. Gains oxygen, or
2. Loses hydrogen

Example:

2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO

Copper gains oxygen and is therefore oxidised.

Another example:

CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O

In this reaction, hydrogen gains oxygen and is oxidised.  

---

TOPIC: Reduction

A substance is said to be reduced when it:

1. Loses oxygen, or
2. Gains hydrogen

Example:

CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O

Copper oxide loses oxygen and is therefore reduced.

---

TOPIC: Redox Reaction

A reaction in which oxidation and reduction occur
simultaneously is called an oxidation-reduction reaction or
a redox reaction.

Example:

CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O

CuO is reduced to Cu.

H₂ is oxidised to H₂O.

Therefore, oxidation and reduction occur together. 

---

TOPIC: Corrosion

When a metal is attacked by substances in its surroundings,
such as moisture and acids, the process is called corrosion.

Examples:

1. Rusting of iron
2. Black coating on silver
3. Green coating on copper

Corrosion damages objects such as:

* Car bodies
* Bridges
* Iron railings
* Ships
* Other metal objects



---

TOPIC: Rusting

Rusting is the corrosion of iron.

Iron articles may develop a reddish-brown coating when left
exposed to suitable surroundings for some time.

This coating is commonly called rust. 

---

TOPIC: Rancidity

When fats and oils are oxidised, they become rancid.

Their smell and taste change.

Rancidity can be slowed down by:

1. Adding antioxidants to food.
2. Keeping food in airtight containers.
3. Flushing packets containing fats and oils with nitrogen.

Nitrogen is used in packets such as chips to prevent oxidation.

## IMPORTANT SUMMARY

**Chemical Reaction:**
A process in which the nature and identity of substances change,
forming new substances.

**Chemical Equation:**
A symbolic representation of a chemical reaction.

**Balanced Chemical Equation:**
An equation in which the number of atoms of each element is the
same on both sides.

**Combination Reaction:**
Two or more substances combine to form one product.

**Decomposition Reaction:**
One substance breaks down to form two or more substances.

**Exothermic Reaction:**
A reaction that releases heat or energy.

**Endothermic Reaction:**
A reaction that absorbs energy.

**Displacement Reaction:**
A more reactive element displaces another element from its
compound.

**Double Displacement Reaction:**
Ions or groups of atoms are exchanged between reactants.

**Precipitation Reaction:**
A reaction that produces an insoluble salt or precipitate.

**Oxidation:**
Gain of oxygen or loss of hydrogen.

**Reduction:**
Loss of oxygen or gain of hydrogen.

**Redox Reaction:**
Oxidation and reduction occur simultaneously.

**Corrosion:**
Damage to a metal due to substances such as moisture or acids.

**Rancidity:**
Oxidation of fats and oils, causing changes in their smell and
taste.
