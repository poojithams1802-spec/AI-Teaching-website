TOPIC: Prime Factorisation

Prime factorisation means expressing a natural or composite number
as a product of prime numbers.

Example:

32760 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 5 × 7 × 13

Therefore:

32760 = 2³ × 3² × 5 × 7 × 13

Prime factors can be written in the form of powers of primes. 

---

TOPIC: Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic

Every composite number can be expressed as a product of primes.

This prime factorisation is unique, except for the order in which
the prime factors occur.

Example:

2 × 3 × 5 × 7

and

3 × 5 × 7 × 2

represent the same prime factorisation.

Therefore:

The prime factorisation of a natural number is unique,
except for the order of its factors. 

---

TOPIC: General Form of Prime Factorisation

A composite number can be written as:

x = p₁ × p₂ × ... × pₙ

where:

p₁, p₂, ..., pₙ are prime numbers.

When the same prime factors are combined, the number can be
written using powers.

Example:

32760 = 2³ × 3² × 5 × 7 × 13 

---

TOPIC: Application of the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic

The uniqueness of prime factorisation can be used to solve
problems involving factors and divisibility.

Example:

Can 4ⁿ end with the digit 0 for any natural number n?

4ⁿ = (2²)ⁿ = 2²ⁿ

Thus, the only prime factor of 4ⁿ is 2.

A number ending in 0 must be divisible by 5.

Since the prime factorisation of 4ⁿ does not contain 5:

4ⁿ cannot end with the digit 0 for any natural number n. 

---

TOPIC: HCF by Prime Factorisation

The HCF is found by taking the smallest power of each
common prime factor.

Example:

6 = 2¹ × 3¹

20 = 2² × 5¹

The common prime factor is 2.

Therefore:

HCF(6, 20) = 2¹ = 2

HCF = Product of the smallest powers of the common
prime factors. 

---

TOPIC: LCM by Prime Factorisation

The LCM is found by taking the greatest power of every
prime factor involved in the numbers.

Example:

6 = 2¹ × 3¹

20 = 2² × 5¹

Therefore:

LCM(6, 20) = 2² × 3¹ × 5¹

LCM(6, 20) = 60

LCM = Product of the greatest powers of all the
prime factors involved. 

---

TOPIC: Relation Between HCF and LCM of Two Numbers

For any two positive integers a and b:

HCF(a, b) × LCM(a, b) = a × b

This formula can be used to find the LCM when the HCF
of two numbers is known.

Example:

HCF(96, 404) = 4

Therefore:

LCM(96, 404) = (96 × 404) / 4

LCM(96, 404) = 9696  

---

TOPIC: HCF and LCM of Three Numbers

Example:

6 = 2 × 3

72 = 2³ × 3²

120 = 2³ × 3 × 5

For HCF, take the smallest powers of the common factors:

HCF(6, 72, 120) = 2 × 3 = 6

For LCM, take the greatest powers of all prime factors:

LCM(6, 72, 120) = 2³ × 3² × 5

LCM(6, 72, 120) = 360 

---

TOPIC: Important Note on Three Numbers

For three positive integers:

HCF(p, q, r) × LCM(p, q, r) ≠ p × q × r

Therefore, the relation:

HCF × LCM = Product of the numbers

is valid for two positive integers, but not generally in the same
form for three numbers.

TOPIC: Irrational Number

A number is called irrational if it cannot be written in the form:

p/q

where:

p and q are integers

q ≠ 0

Examples of irrational numbers include:

√2

√3

√5

π

Irrational numbers, together with rational numbers, form the
set of real numbers. 

---

TOPIC: Theorem on Prime Divisibility

If p is a prime number and p divides a², then p divides a,
where a is a positive integer.

Mathematically:

If p | a², then p | a

This result follows from the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic
and the uniqueness of prime factorisation.  

---

TOPIC: Proof by Contradiction

Proof by contradiction is a technique in which we:

1. Assume that the statement is false or assume the opposite.
2. Use this assumption to derive a contradiction.
3. Conclude that the original assumption was incorrect.
4. Therefore, the required statement is true.

This method is used in the chapter to prove that numbers such as
√2 and √3 are irrational. 

---

TOPIC: Proof that √2 is Irrational

Assume that √2 is rational.

Then:

√2 = a/b

where a and b are coprime integers and b ≠ 0.

Squaring both sides:

2 = a²/b²

Therefore:

a² = 2b²

Thus, 2 divides a².

By the prime divisibility theorem:

2 divides a

Therefore:

a = 2c

for some integer c.

Substituting:

2b² = 4c²

Therefore:

b² = 2c²

Thus, 2 divides b², and hence 2 divides b.

Therefore, both a and b have 2 as a common factor.

This contradicts the assumption that a and b are coprime.

Hence:

√2 is irrational. 

---

TOPIC: Proof that √3 is Irrational

Assume that √3 is rational.

Then:

√3 = a/b

where a and b are coprime.

Squaring both sides:

3b² = a²

Therefore, 3 divides a².

By the prime divisibility theorem:

3 divides a

So:

a = 3c

for some integer c.

Substituting:

3b² = 9c²

Therefore:

b² = 3c²

Thus, 3 divides b² and hence 3 divides b.

Therefore, both a and b have 3 as a common factor.

This contradicts the fact that a and b are coprime.

Hence:

√3 is irrational.  

---

TOPIC: Sum or Difference of Rational and Irrational Numbers

The chapter states that:

The sum or difference of a rational number and an irrational number
is irrational.

Example:

√5 − √3

Assume:

√5 − √3

is rational.

Then, after rearranging:

√3 = √5 − a/b

The right-hand side would be rational.

This would imply that √3 is rational, which is a contradiction.

Therefore:

√5 − √3 is irrational. 

---

TOPIC: Product of a Non-Zero Rational Number and an Irrational Number

The chapter states that the product of a non-zero rational number
and an irrational number is irrational.

Example:

3√2

Assume that:

3√2

is rational.

Then:

√2 = a/(3b)

This would make √2 rational.

But √2 is irrational.

This is a contradiction.

Therefore:

3√2 is irrational.

**Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic:**

Every composite number can be expressed as a product of primes,
and this factorisation is unique apart from the order of the
prime factors.

**Prime Divisibility Theorem:**

If p is a prime number and p divides a², then p divides a.

**HCF of Two Numbers:**

Product of the smallest powers of the common prime factors.

**LCM of Numbers:**

Product of the greatest powers of all prime factors involved.

**Relation for Two Numbers:**

HCF(a, b) × LCM(a, b) = a × b

**Irrational Number:**

A number that cannot be written in the form p/q, where p and q
are integers and q ≠ 0.

**Proof by Contradiction:**

A method in which an opposite assumption is shown to lead to
a contradiction.
